# Sēn — Validation Plan V1

## Objective
Validate the company thesis before scaling software, supply or paid acquisition.

Sēn must prove:
1. People will pay for cross-category access.
2. Partners will release useful inventory at viable economics.
3. Members can understand and use the product without founder explanation.
4. Sēn AI reduces friction enough to matter.
5. Operations stay reliable at small scale.
6. Repeat usage exists beyond novelty.

---

## Research sequence

### Phase A — Founder-led qualitative validation
Target:
- 12–15 member interviews
- 8–10 partner discovery conversations

Purpose:
- understand current behavior
- understand spending
- identify strongest job-to-be-done
- identify dealbreakers
- test language and positioning

### Phase B — Prototype usability
Target:
- 8–12 users

Tasks:
1. Find a reformer option tomorrow after 18h.
2. Understand Included vs Member Price.
3. Book a class.
4. Find and use a Privilege.
5. Ask Sēn for an evening recommendation.
6. Join a Squad and explain whether that reserves inventory.
7. Recover from a partner cancellation.

Measure:
- task completion
- time to completion
- hesitation
- confusion
- false assumptions
- need for moderator help

### Phase C — Willingness-to-pay
Do not ask only “would you pay?”

Use:
- current monthly spend
- forced-choice price points
- plan choice
- cancellation trigger
- commitment behavior

Key question:
“At which monthly price would you seriously consider subscribing if the launch network had the places you actually use?”

### Phase D — Founding 25
Convert from interview intent to real commitment.

The strongest evidence hierarchy:
1. Pays
2. Deposits / commits
3. Joins waitlist with specific launch conditions
4. Books test experiences
5. Says “I would use this”
6. Says “cool idea”

Only levels 1–3 should materially influence go/no-go decisions.

---

## Member sample

Prioritize:
- adults in Brasília
- existing paid fitness/wellness behavior
- at least R$300/month current category spend
- users across Core / Plus / Black affordability bands
- mix of heavy studio users and broader wellness users
- people who already coordinate multiple providers

Avoid over-indexing on:
- friends who will say yes to be supportive
- people who rarely pay for wellness
- people joining only for free access

---

## Partner sample

Start with P0 candidates across:
- premium gym
- reformer
- boutique fitness
- recovery
- coworking
- selected beauty

Need both:
- owner/founder-led businesses
- more structured multi-unit operators

## Core member questions
1. Walk me through what you paid for last month.
2. What do you wish were easier?
3. Which memberships do you already have?
4. What do you stop using first when life gets busy?
5. Which part of Sēn feels essential?
6. Which part feels unnecessary?
7. What would make you distrust it?
8. What would make you cancel after month one?
9. Which plan would you pick?
10. At what price does that plan become hard to justify?

---

## Core partner questions
1. Which capacity is underutilized?
2. What capacity is strategically protected?
3. What does a good external member look like?
4. Which existing aggregator relationships exist?
5. Are there exclusivity restrictions?
6. What payout model feels fair?
7. What creates check-in friction?
8. What causes no-show pain?
9. Which data/reporting matters?
10. What would cause you to terminate a pilot?

## Validation thresholds before pilot

### Demand
- At least 40% of qualified interviewed users express serious consideration at provisional pricing.
- At least 25% of qualified users show a stronger commitment signal than “sounds good.”
- At least 3 different categories appear as meaningful reasons to subscribe.

### Usability
- 80%+ complete core booking flow without moderator help.
- 80%+ correctly explain Included vs Member Price.
- 80%+ understand Squad participation does not guarantee booking.
- Near-zero false-success perception in AI transactional flow.

### Supply
- At least 15 high-quality partners are commercially plausible.
- At least 8 are willing to discuss a controlled pilot.
- Reformer + gym + boutique training + one recovery/work layer are represented.

### Economics
- No proposed launch mix knowingly destroys Core/Plus base-case margin.
- Heavy-user risk is understood and Black remains controlled.
- Partner asks do not require broad minimum guarantees.

---

## Red flags
- Most interest is driven only by one cheap category.
- Users like the brand but reject pricing.
- Partners only accept economics that resemble retail pricing.
- Users cannot understand benefit states.
- AI feels decorative rather than useful.
- Support intervention is required for normal bookings.
- Community is interesting but does not lead to offline action.

---

## Decision rule
Do not scale because feedback is enthusiastic.

Scale only when behavior, economics and operational evidence support it.
